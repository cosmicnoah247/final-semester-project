First Stars Overview

The first stars formed from primordial hydrogen and helium, ending the cosmic dark ages and bringing the universe its first visible light. These ancient stars were massive, bright, and short-lived.

Their birth began when gravity pulled together the first gas clouds in regions of slightly higher density. Once the gas reached extreme temperatures, nuclear fusion ignited and stars were born.

These stars had powerful abilities: they fused hydrogen into heavier elements, emitted intense ultraviolet radiation, and reionized the surrounding gas. Their light reshaped the early universe and paved the way for galaxies.

Their death came quickly, often as supernova explosions that spread metals throughout space. Those heavy elements seeded future generations of stars, planets, and ultimately the chemistry needed for life.
