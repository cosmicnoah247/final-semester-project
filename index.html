    [ LIFECYCLE SUMMARY ]

    Solar System

    Birth: Formed from a rotating cloud of gas and dust called the solar nebula about 4.6 billion years ago.
    Facts: Contains eight planets, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets, and the Sun, orbiting around a central star.
    Death: Will end when the Sun expands into a red giant and engulfs inner planets, followed by the Sun becoming a white dwarf.

    Pulsars

    Birth: Created after a massive star explodes in a supernova and its core collapses into a dense neutron star.
    Facts: Rapidly spinning neutron stars that emit beams of radio waves and light, detectable as pulses from Earth.
    Death: Slow down over time due to energy loss and may become radio-quiet neutron stars or evolve into magnetars with extreme magnetic fields.

    Gamma Ray Bursts

    Birth: Triggered by the collapse of massive stars or mergers of compact objects like neutron stars and black holes.
    Facts: The brightest explosions in the universe, releasing enormous energy in gamma rays within seconds, visible across vast distances.
    Death: Fade quickly into afterglows across the electromagnetic spectrum, providing clues about their origins.

    Black Holes

    Birth: Formed from the collapse of massive stars at the end of their life or by merging dense stellar remnants in binary systems.
    Facts: Regions of space with gravity so strong that not even light can escape, defined by their event horizon.
    Death: Shrink gradually via Hawking radiation over incredibly long timescales, potentially evaporating completely.

    Supernova

    Birth: Occurs when a massive star exhausts its nuclear fuel and its core collapses, triggering a catastrophic explosion.
    Facts: Produces heavy elements like iron and gold, and can outshine entire galaxies for weeks due to immense energy release.
    Death: Leaves behind a neutron star or black hole and disperses enriched gas into space, seeding new star formation.

    Kilonova

    Birth: Happens when two neutron stars in a binary system merge, releasing a burst of radioactive material.
    Facts: Creates heavy elements like gold and platinum through rapid neutron capture, and emits a bright optical/infrared glow.
    Death: Fades as the material expands and cools over days to weeks, leaving behind a compact remnant like a black hole or neutron star.

    Supermassive Black Holes

    Birth: Formed through the gradual accretion of gas, dust, and stars over billions of years, or via mergers of smaller black holes.
    Facts: Reside at the centers of most galaxies, with masses ranging from millions to billions of solar masses, influencing galactic evolution.
    Death: May eventually evaporate through Hawking radiation, though this process takes far longer than the current age of the universe.

    Dark Matter

    Birth: Emerged in the early universe, likely as particles produced during the Big Bang or shortly thereafter.
    Facts: Comprises about 27% of the universe's mass-energy content, interacts only through gravity, and is essential for galaxy formation.
    Death: Believed to be stable and long-lived, persisting throughout the universe's history without decay.

    Dark Energy

    Birth: Became dominant after the Big Bang as ordinary matter and radiation diluted with cosmic expansion.
    Facts: Accounts for approximately 68% of the universe's energy content and drives the accelerated expansion of space.
    Death: Will ultimately lead to the universe's heat death, where all energy is evenly distributed and no work can be done.
