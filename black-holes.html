Black Holes

Dive into objects with gravity so strong that not even light escapes.

Black Hole Anatomy

A black hole forms when a massive star collapses and its core becomes infinitely dense. The boundary around it is called the event horizon, beyond which escape is impossible.

Black holes can be stellar, intermediate, or supermassive. Their gravity warps space and time, bending light and affecting surrounding matter to create luminous accretion disks.

Recent observations from gravitational waves and imaging have confirmed black holes as real astrophysical objects. Their behavior tests general relativity in the strongest fields available.

Even though nothing can escape the horizon, black holes can grow by consuming gas and stars. Over extremely long timescales, Hawking radiation may cause the slow evaporation of the smallest black holes.
