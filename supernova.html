Supernova Mechanics

A supernova occurs when a massive star exhausts its fuel and its core collapses, releasing a shockwave that blasts the outer layers into space. These explosions can briefly outshine entire galaxies.

There are two main types: core-collapse supernovae from massive stars and thermonuclear supernovae from white dwarfs in binary systems. Both create heavy elements and send shockwaves through their surroundings.

Supernova remnants are glowing shells of gas and dust that can trigger new star formation. Observations of supernovae also help astronomers measure cosmic distances and understand stellar evolution.

The explosive release of energy can leave behind neutron stars, pulsars, or black holes, depending on the original star's mass. These outcomes connect supernovae to many other extreme cosmic objects.
