Dark Matter Insights

Dark matter is a mysterious form of matter that does not emit, absorb, or reflect light, making it invisible to telescopes. It constitutes about 27% of the universe's total mass-energy content and is essential for understanding cosmic structure.

Dark matter formed in the early universe, likely as particles created during the Big Bang or shortly thereafter. Unlike ordinary matter, it does not interact with electromagnetic forces, only gravity, allowing it to pass through normal matter undetected.

This invisible substance provides the gravitational scaffolding that holds galaxies and galaxy clusters together. Without dark matter, galaxies would fly apart due to their observed rotation speeds, and large-scale structures would not form.

Dark matter particles are believed to be stable and long-lived, persisting throughout the universe's history. While we detect its effects through gravity, its exact nature remains one of physics' greatest unsolved mysteries.
