Pulsar Science

Pulsars are rotating neutron stars born from supernova explosions. Their enormous density and strong magnetic fields produce beams of radio and light that sweep past observers as regular pulses.

Some pulsars spin hundreds of times per second, making them precise cosmic clocks. Astronomers use them to study gravitational fields, test relativity, and search for gravitational waves.

These objects are typically just 20 kilometers across but contain more mass than the Sun. Their radiation can appear in radio, X-ray, and gamma-ray wavelengths depending on the pulsar type.

As pulsars lose energy, their spin slows and their emission dims. Over time they may turn into silent neutron stars or become magnetars if their magnetic fields remain extreme.
