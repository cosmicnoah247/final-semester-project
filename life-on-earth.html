Life on Earth Overview

Life on Earth emerged approximately 3.7 billion years ago in the planet's primordial oceans. The first organisms were likely simple, single-celled microbes that harnessed chemical energy from their environment.

These early life forms evolved through abiogenesis - the natural process by which life arises from non-living matter. Volcanic activity, lightning, and ultraviolet radiation provided the energy needed for complex organic molecules to form and self-organize.

Photosynthesis revolutionized Earth's biosphere around 2.5 billion years ago, producing oxygen and enabling aerobic respiration. This "Great Oxygenation Event" transformed the atmosphere and paved the way for more complex life forms.

From these humble beginnings, life diversified through natural selection, leading to the incredible variety of organisms we see today. Earth's biosphere continues to evolve, adapting to changing environmental conditions over billions of years.
