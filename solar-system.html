Solar System Overview

The Solar System formed around 4.6 billion years ago from a collapsing nebula of gas and dust. The central protostar became the Sun, while surrounding material clumped into planets, moons, asteroids, and comets.

Gravity shaped the system into a structure with rocky inner planets and giant gaseous worlds farther out. The asteroid belt and Kuiper belt preserve the remains of the early formation process.

The Sun provides virtually all the energy for the system, driving weather, climate, and chemical cycles on the planets. Solar wind and magnetic activity also shape the heliosphere and space weather.

Far into the future, the Sun will expand into a red giant and transform the inner Solar System. That evolution marks the long-term destiny of the system and its remaining worlds.
