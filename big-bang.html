Big Bang Overview

The Big Bang marks the beginning of the universe, when space, time, matter, and energy emerged from an incredibly hot, dense state. This event set the stage for everything we observe today.

In the first fractions of a second, the universe expanded faster than the speed of light during cosmic inflation. Particles and radiation formed as the energy cooled, creating the building blocks of atoms.

The Big Bang's "cool abilities" include making the first elementary particles, producing the cosmic microwave background radiation, and generating the initial fluctuations that would grow into galaxies and clusters.

There is no death in the traditional sense, but the initial singularity phase ended as the universe expanded and cooled. From that first explosion came the continuous evolution of space into the cosmos we know.
