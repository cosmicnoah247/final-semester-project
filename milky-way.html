Milky Way Formation

The Milky Way formed as gas, dust, and dark matter merged into a massive spiral galaxy. Its growth began billions of years ago when the first galaxy fragments assembled under gravity.

This galaxy's birth involved the accretion of smaller protogalaxies and the settling of gas into a rotating disk. The central bulge and stellar halo also formed from early mergers and rapid star formation.

The Milky Way's "cool abilities" include hosting a diverse population of stars, supporting star formation in its spiral arms, and holding an enormous dark matter halo that shapes its rotation and structure.

The Milky Way's future death will arrive as it merges with the Andromeda galaxy, reshaping both into a larger elliptical system. Over many billions of years, the galaxy will settle into a quieter state as star formation slows.
