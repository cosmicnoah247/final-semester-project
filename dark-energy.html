Dark Energy Phenomena

Dark energy is a hypothetical form of energy that permeates all of space and drives the accelerated expansion of the universe. It accounts for about 68% of the universe's total energy content and is responsible for the cosmos' increasing rate of growth.

Dark energy emerged as a dominant force after the Big Bang, becoming significant as ordinary matter and radiation diluted with expansion. Its effects became apparent in the late 1990s through observations of distant supernovae.

This mysterious energy acts as a repulsive force, countering gravity on cosmic scales. It causes space itself to expand at an accelerating rate, pushing galaxies apart faster over time and shaping the universe's large-scale structure.

Dark energy will ultimately lead to the universe's heat death, where all energy is evenly distributed and no work can be done. This fate, known as the Big Freeze, represents the long-term destiny of an accelerating cosmos.
