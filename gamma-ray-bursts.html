Gamma Ray Burst Insights

Gamma ray bursts are intense flashes of high-energy photons that appear from catastrophic cosmic events. They are among the most luminous explosions known, visible across billions of light-years.

Short bursts likely come from neutron star mergers, while long bursts are linked to the collapse of massive stars. The initial flash is followed by an afterglow that reveals the environment around the explosion.

The energy released in a single burst can exceed what the Sun emits over its entire life. Because they occur at extreme distances, GRBs also act as beacons probing the early universe.

Researchers analyze burst spectra and afterglows to understand jet structure, magnetic fields, and element formation. GRBs are key to studying both stellar death and cosmological evolution.
